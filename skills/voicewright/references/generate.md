# Generating lines

- Line list: JSON or CSV of `id`, `voice`, `text`, `say`, `direction`, `emotion`. `text` is for subtitles, `say` is for the engine. Name clips `<voice>_<id>_t<take>.wav`. A game dialogue file is converted to this list, never edited.
- Pronunciation: respell in `say` to a spelling with one reading ("bough" for a ship's bow). Speech-to-text writes words, not sounds, so it passes the wrong homograph; check the vowel with a phoneme recogniser or by ear.
- Normalise: in `say`, write numbers, dates, money, abbreviations, symbols and web addresses as the words to be spoken. An unusual string is a first suspect when a line fails.
- Writing `say` for the ear: punctuation in `text` is for the eye. Put a pause (dash or ellipsis) just before the word that matters, one or two a line at most. Render with and without on one seed and keep what the ear prefers; engines read marks differently.
- Chunks: engines drift and speed up on long text and break on very short lines. Stay under about 20 s or 350 characters a chunk; pad a one-word line inside a phrase and cut it out. Cut at sentence ends, or at phrase ends inside a long sentence. A chunk rendered alone loses the sentences around it, so listen across the joins for resets in pitch and pace.
- References: 5-30 s of dry speech, one clear delivery, nothing under it; room sound in a reference carries into every take. In-context clones copy the reference's delivery; for a shout use embedding-only mode (Qwen3 `x_vector`) and one reference per emotion.
- Direction: one sentence each for the event the line answers (one specific thing just said or done, not a mood), who it is said to, and the thought under the words. Long director's notes are a known cause of voice drift, so keep identity out of them. A flat or wrong read has three levers, where the engine exposes them: the reference (a clip with that delivery), the text (wording that implies the feeling) and the seed. A requested emotion may simply be ignored, so judge by ear.
- Dialogue: render each line alone with frozen voices and join, unless a test proves a native multi-speaker engine holds its voices. Timing lives in the join: set each gap by hand, short for a picked-up cue, about a second for a beat, overlap or a cut-off tail for an interruption.
- Batches: use the idle host; on a busy shared GPU queue behind it and wait. `engines/qwen3.py jobs.json` runs a resumable batch.
- Review: `VW review`; note heard or unheard for each clip.
- Retry notes: name one move, not a mood: no stress on the phrase, the opposite feeling, a new thought under the line, a pause at one named place. For an important line render three takes with three different thoughts.
- First test by fault: a skipped or repeated word or garbled stretch points at alignment, so diff a transcript against the text, shorten the chunk, simplify the text, try a new seed. Hoarse, metallic or pitch-breaking sound points at the waveform stage, so rewording is less likely to help: try another seed or engine.
- Direction, `say` writing, timing and retry notes come from human voice-acting craft (Alburger), the fault split from a 2023 textbook on older models (Tan). None has been tested on current engines: they pick what to try, and a blind listen decides.
