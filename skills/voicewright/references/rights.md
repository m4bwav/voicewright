# Rights, consent, disclosure

Not legal advice. Checked 2026-11-04; re-check before relying on it. Detail and sources: repository `ai-docs/research/2026-11-04-craft-reception-law.md`.

- **Consent.** Copy the union standard: separate written consent, specific uses, a record kept with the voice. Applies to family and to the user's own voice used by others.
- **Sound-alikes.** Refuse imitation of an identifiable person. ELVIS (Tennessee) and California turn on "readily identifiable", so a designed voice is not safe by default if it happens to resemble someone; whether that reaches accidental likeness is unsettled.
- **Ownership.** Copyright does not protect a voice as such (Lehrman v. Lovo, 2025). Never promise exclusivity.
- **NO FAKES** is not law: committee 2026-06-18, unanimous consent blocked 2026-09-30.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50** has applied since 2026-08-02: disclose deepfakes, with a lighter discreet credit for fiction. Whether a realistic designed voice counts is unsettled.
- **Steam:** disclose AI voice that ships or appears in marketing; live generation also needs guardrails. Re-read the form: it changed in January 2026.
- **ACX:** human narration unless authorized.
- **Truth.** Never write "no AI" or a false narrator credit. Check vendors and localization too.
- **Reception.** Consent does not stop backlash. Offer AI for temp and minor lines, and humans for hero lines where the audience cares; Arc Raiders re-recorded some lines by March 2026 after criticism.
- **Provenance.** Per voice: model, version, licence, plan, seed, settings, source clips, date. No record, no ship.
